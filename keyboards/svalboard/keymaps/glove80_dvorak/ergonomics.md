# Svalboard Key Accessibility Classes

Ergonomic accessibility ratings for each key position on the Svalboard,
based on finger strength, direction biomechanics, and user experience reports.

Each finger sits in a key well with 5 directions: center (press down),
north (push away), south (pull toward), and two lateral directions.
"Inward" means toward the body center (right-hand west, left-hand east);
"outward" means away from the body center (right-hand east, left-hand west).

Classes: **S** effortless | **A** easy | **B** comfortable | **C** moderate effort | **D** rare keys only | **F** avoid

## Right Hand

East = outward (abduction), West = inward (adduction).

| Finger | Center | South | West (in) | North | East (out) | Double |
|--------|--------|-------|-----------|-------|------------|--------|
| R1 Index | S | S | A | A | B | D |
| R2 Middle | S | A | A | B | C | D |
| R3 Ring | B | B | B | C | D | F |
| R4 Pinky | C | C | D | D | F | F |

## Left Hand

East = inward (adduction), West = outward (abduction).

| Finger | Center | South | East (in) | North | West (out) | Double |
|--------|--------|-------|-----------|-------|------------|--------|
| L1 Index | S | S | A | A | B | D |
| L2 Middle | S | A | A | B | C | D |
| L3 Ring | B | B | B | C | D | F |
| L4 Pinky | C | C | D | D | F | F |

## Thumb Cluster

| Position | Class | Notes |
|----------|-------|-------|
| Down | A | Natural downward press |
| Pad | A | Inward roll, very natural |
| Knuckle | B | Accessible but awkward angle |
| Up | C | Causes strain with heavy use |
| Nail | C | Extension, similar strain risk |
| DoubleDown | D | Two sequential presses, slowest |

## Sources

- Direction hierarchy: center > south (flexion) > inward lateral > north (extension) > outward lateral > double
- Finger hierarchy: index > middle > ring > pinky
- Thumb strain reports: "Up" and "Nail" positions cause end-of-day pain if assigned to frequently-used keys
- Svalboard key travel: 2mm lateral, 1.7mm center; 20g magneto-optical switches
- References: svalboard.com docs, twey.io/hci/svalboard, DataHand ergonomic studies, r/ErgoMechKeyboards user reports
